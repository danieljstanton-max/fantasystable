Common questions

Is it illegal for me to play, or only to run a casino?
Both. The National Gambling Act makes it an offence to engage in interactive gambling as well as to offer it. Prosecutions of individual players are rare, but the law does not only target operators, and winnings from an unlawful bet have been forfeited in South African courts.
Betway and other bookmakers offer slots. Is that legal?
Not any more. Licensed South African bookmakers offered casino-style games as fixed-odds bets under a sports betting licence. In February 2026 the National Gambling Board issued a formal notice that remote gambling servers are unlawful, and that a bookmaker may not offer these games without a casino licence, which no online operator holds.
When will online casinos become legal in South Africa?
No date has been set. The Remote Gambling Bill of 2024 and the earlier National Gambling Amendment Bill both failed to advance. The Portfolio Committee on Trade, Industry and Competition is exploring a committee bill instead. Anyone quoting a launch date is guessing.
Do I pay tax if I win?
Casual winnings are not taxed in South Africa, because they are treated as capital rather than income. That does not make an illegal bet legal, and someone gambling as a trade can be assessed on the profits.

Kenya has the most developed online casino market in East Africa and, since 2025, one of the most heavily rebuilt regulatory regimes on the continent.

The Gambling Control Act 2025 replaced legislation dating to the 1960s. It raised capital requirements sharply, required local incorporation with at least 30% Kenyan ownership, and created the Gambling Regulatory Authority, which took over from the Betting Control and Licensing Board in February 2026. Any guide still describing the BCLB as Kenya's current regulator was written before that handover.

The tax position changed at the same time and is the thing most worth understanding before you play. Kenya now charges 5% excise on deposits and 5% withholding on withdrawals, replacing the old 15% excise and 20% tax on net winnings. For a casual player who deposits occasionally, the new regime is cheaper. For anyone cycling money in and out of a wallet frequently, it is considerably more expensive, because the charge attaches to the transfer rather than to any winnings.

Morocco licenses gambling in its land-based forms — casinos, horse racing and sports betting all operate under state licences — and has never built a framework for the online version.

That did not stop it taxing the online version. The 2025 Finance Bill introduced a 30% withholding on winnings obtained through foreign online gambling platforms, with a further 2% solidarity contribution on the same sum. The combined 32% is among the heaviest player-level charges anywhere on this list, and it applies to activity the state does not license.

The practical trap is that a foreign operator cannot withhold Moroccan tax and will not try. You will be paid in full, and the liability will still be yours.

Common questions

Is online gambling legal in Morocco?
There is no legal framework for it. Casinos, horse racing and sports betting are licensed by the state in their land-based forms, but online gambling has never been brought within a licensing regime.
Why does Morocco tax something it does not license?
Because the tax attaches to the player, not the operator. The 2025 Finance Bill imposed a 30% withholding on winnings from foreign online gambling platforms, plus a 2% solidarity contribution — a total of 32% on money the state has no other way of reaching.
Will the operator deduct the Moroccan tax for me?
No. A foreign operator has no mechanism to withhold Moroccan tax and will pay you gross. The obligation remains yours, which is the part most guides omit.

How we rate online casinos

In short

Every operator is scored on four things we can check: whether the licence is real, how fast a withdrawal actually arrives, what the bonus terms say when you read past the headline, and whether support answers. Nothing else moves a rating — not commission, not a relationship, not how much traffic an operator sends us.

Where we cannot check something, we leave it blank and say so. That is why most payout columns on this site currently read "not tested".

We check the register, not the badge

A licence badge in a site footer is an image. Anyone can make one. The only thing that counts is the entry on the regulator's own register, so that is where we look — the KwaZulu-Natal Gaming and Betting Board, the Gaming Commission of Ghana, the Gambling Regulatory Authority in Kenya, and so on.

When we have seen the entry, the operator's page carries the licence number and the date we checked it. When we have not, it says so plainly as a weakness. Of the operators listed on this site, a minority currently have a number we have verified. We would rather show you which is which than imply we checked all of them.

The rating is out of five, and it is arithmetic

Licence status, tested payout speed, bonus terms and support quality. An operator with no verifiable licence cannot score above three, whatever else it does well. An operator we have not been able to test a withdrawal at cannot score above four and a half.

Position in any list on this site is computed from that number, descending. It is never set by hand. An operator that has not been rated does not appear at all, which is why some markets here show fewer names than you will find elsewhere.

Payout times stay blank until we time one

Every comparison site claims fast payouts. Almost none of them say when they last checked, or what they measured.

We deposit, we play, we withdraw, and we record the time from clicking withdraw to the money arriving. Until we have done that for a given operator, the column reads "not tested" in red. It is the least flattering thing on this site and the most useful, because it tells you exactly how much of what you are reading is evidence and how much is not.

Every review names at least three things the operator does badly

This is a rule, not a habit. If we could not find three genuine weaknesses, we had not looked hard enough to publish.

It applies to the operators we earn the most from as much as to the ones we earn nothing from. Hollywoodbets has the best-documented licence on the site and its review still says the interface is dated and the welcome offer is small.

How we are paid, and what it does not buy

We earn commission when a reader opens an account through a link on this site. Some operators pay more than others.

It buys a listing, provided the operator meets the licensing bar. It does not buy a position, a rating, a softened weakness, or the removal of a warning. Position is computed from the rating; the rating comes from the four checks above. If an operator wants to move up, the route is to pay withdrawals faster.

Sometimes we recommend nobody

Egypt is the clearest case. Online gambling is illegal for residents, sites have been blocked since February 2026, and criminalisation is before parliament. We could list five offshore operators there and earn from it. We do not, because a reader following that advice would be reaching sites that are blocked, with no regulator to complain to and a legal position that is getting worse.

South Africa is the harder case. Online casino is unlawful; sports betting is licensed and lawful. We list licensed bookmakers, describe the dispute over their casino sections honestly, and let readers decide.

Tell us when we are wrong

Regulation moves faster than most affiliate sites update. Kenya changed regulator in February 2026, Zimbabwe introduced a 25% tax in January, Ghana abolished its tax in April 2025. Each of those is on our record of changes with the date we logged it.

If something here is out of date or wrong, tell us and we will check it against the primary source and correct it with a note. That record is on the homepage so you can see how often it happens.

Questions about how we work

Do operators pay to appear on this site?
They pay commission when a reader opens an account, and that is all it buys. Position is computed from the rating, which comes from licence status, tested payout speed, bonus terms and support. No operator can pay to move up, soften a weakness, or remove a warning.
Why do so many payout times say "not tested"?
Because we have not tested them yet, and saying so is more useful than guessing. Every other comparison site claims fast payouts without stating when they last checked. Ours will fill in as we work through them, and each figure will carry the date it was measured.
Why are there fewer casinos here than on other sites?
An operator with no rating does not appear at all. Sites listing seventy or a hundred casinos are listing everything that pays commission. We list what we have assessed, which is a smaller number and a more useful one.
How often do you re-check licences?
Monthly, and every page carries the date it was last verified. If a date on this site is more than a couple of months old, treat the licence claim beside it with suspicion and check the register yourself — the link is on every country page.
